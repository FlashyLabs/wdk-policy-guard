# Code of Conduct

## The standard

Be decent. Assume the person on the other side is acting in good faith, is
working with less context than you have, and is worth the extra sentence it
takes to explain rather than dismiss.

Concretely, in this project's issues, pull requests and discussions:

- Critique the work, never the person. "This drops the consent check" is
  useful. "This is careless" is not, and says less.
- Explain the *why*. A review that says only "wrong" costs the author a day and
  teaches them nothing.
- Take disagreement to evidence. Most arguments here are settleable by a test,
  a spec, or a measurement — reach for one before reaching for volume.
- Newcomers get patience. Somebody's first pull request is the cheapest moment
  to make this a project people want to return to.

## Not acceptable

Harassment, personal attacks, discriminatory or demeaning remarks, sustained
disruption, publishing anyone's private information, or unwelcome attention of a
sexual nature. Also: pressuring a contributor to bypass a security or consent
rule, which is a safety issue here rather than merely a rude one.

## Scope

Project spaces — repositories, issues, pull requests, discussions — and any
place someone is representing the project in public.

## Enforcement

Report to **conduct@flashy.network**. Reports go to a human, are held in
confidence, and are acknowledged within 72 hours.

Responses run from a private word, through a warning, to a temporary or
permanent ban. What we will not do is quietly ignore a report because the person
reported is a productive contributor — that trade is how a project ends up with
a reputation nobody will say out loud.

If your report concerns the person who reads that inbox, send it to
**security@flashy.network** instead and say so.
